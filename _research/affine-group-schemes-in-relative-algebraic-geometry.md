Classically, the spectrum functor induces an equivalence of categories \\( \mathrm{Spec}\colon (\mathrm{CAlg}\_R)^{\mathrm{op}} \xrightarrow{\sim} \mathrm{Aff}\_R \\) between commutative \\( R \\)-algebras and affine schemes over \\( R \\). Relative algebraic geometry generalises this story by working relative to a symmetric monoidal category \\( (\mathcal{C}, \otimes, \mathbf{1}) \\), defining the category of affine objects to be \\( \mathrm{Aff}\_{\mathcal{C}} := \mathrm{Comm}(\mathcal{C})^{\mathrm{op}} \\), the opposite of the category of commutative monoid objects in \\( \mathcal{C} \\). Following the theory developed by Toën–Vaquié, we study how affine group schemes and their antiequivalent commutative Hopf algebras behave in this generality.
